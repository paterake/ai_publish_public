An agent harness taught me why analogies aren’t “dumbing down”.

Early on, the harness would do the wrong thing with total confidence: it would optimise for wording when what we needed was evidence, or it would stop early because the output sounded plausible.

What fixed it wasn’t more philosophy.
It was a handful of analogies that conveyed intent.

“The quality gate is an editor” did real work:
an editor rejects because the evidence is thin, not because the prose needs polish.
So when the gate failed, the instruction was unambiguous: go back to research, don’t reword.

That’s what I mean by analogies as infrastructure.
They’re not decoration. They’re a shared vocabulary that tells humans and agents what to optimise for — and what to treat as failure.

A practical bar I now use:
if you can’t express a core concept in one grounded analogy that assigns responsibility correctly and doesn’t hide the primary failure mode, you probably don’t understand it well enough to ship the explanation.
