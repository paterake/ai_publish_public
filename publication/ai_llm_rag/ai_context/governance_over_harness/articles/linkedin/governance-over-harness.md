The harness is a commodity. The advantage is the governance layer.

The community just figured out that the model isn’t the differentiator — the harness is.

Then claw-code appeared within days.

That’s the moment the next conclusion becomes obvious:
the harness is a commodity too.

If a harness can be cloned quickly, the compounding investment isn’t “which tool”.
It’s the governance layer: the project-specific, git-backed rules, process discipline, and contracts that tell any harness how to behave.

In practice, that means contracts + workflow triggers + executable gates.
Not more instructions in chat.

The consequence is practical, not philosophical:

📌 Swap tools, swap machines, reset sessions — and the behaviour still holds because it’s enforced by what the repo contains, not what the assistant remembers.

I know because this setup runs across three assistants today: the “how we work” rules live once in the repo, and every tool points at the same source of truth.

The test is simple: if you switched your primary AI coding assistant tomorrow, how much of your setup transfers?

If the answer is “little”, you’re investing in the harness.
If the answer is “nearly everything”, you’re investing in governance.

Question: what part of your current setup is still trapped inside one tool?
